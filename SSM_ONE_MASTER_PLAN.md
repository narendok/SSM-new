## 1. Product and data authority

SSM One manages company people, projects, departmental procedures, tasks, controlled records and documents through one connected portal with an Android companion. Authenticated web/backend services are authoritative for identity, membership, settings, permissions, numbering, operational records, approvals, audit history and private files. Android reads and writes the same authorized records through verified contracts; it is not a second source of truth.

Use stable IDs and explicit links between enquiry, opportunity, project, phase, stage, task, record, document revision, asset and inventory item. Settings and templates are versioned. A project pins the accepted lifecycle, department and RACI versions. Changes apply to new work by default; existing projects change only through a reviewed, auditable update. Never silently rewrite signed, completed or historical records.

Searchable selectors and record editors must use canonical master IDs for Customer, Supplier, Project, Part and Product links. Resolve legacy IDs/references only when all populated aliases point to the same exact, authorized master; never infer an identity from a label or title. An explicit edit replaces or clears the full alias family together, and the server validates changed links for active status, organization and caller visibility. Leave untouched historical or unresolved aliases as-is and flag them for review rather than guessing. Web and Android consume the same saved identities and backend rules; platform-specific plans own their UI and verification only.

## 2. Organization, roles and people management

The initial department registry is SAL, EHW, FW, SW, MECH, PUR, QA, EMF, MMF, FIN, SIT and HR/OPS. It is configurable, not a permanent company limit. CFT and PM belong to Operations. MECH owns mechanical design; MMF owns mechanical manufacturing and the confirmed enclosure/3D manufacturing stages. EMF owns electronics manufacturing and EOL testing as a distinct configurable sub-role/work scope; TST is historical, not an active thirteenth department. Keep old TST record IDs and links intact. Service and IT may share a configured workspace while retaining distinct work registers and permissions.

Manage employees through authenticated people records linked to their actual role, department membership, manager, project/team assignments, skills or competency, training and onboarding status. HR/OPS owns confidential people records and applicable company policies. A department merge changes workspace grouping and routing only; it does not merge business records or grant access. RACI assignments never grant data access. Department, role and team changes require server-side authorization, audit history and explicit handling of pending work.

Employee self-service includes the person's permitted profile details, assigned work, training/policy acknowledgements, forms, requests, assigned assets and released personal letters. Web manages authorized templates and campaigns; Android provides the same person's everyday view and actions against shared IDs. HR letters are generated from approved templates and verified employee fields, reviewed before issue, and retained as versioned records. Policies, training and recurring forms bind completion to the exact published version. Survey anonymity, audience and response visibility must be explicit. Chat, discussion, poll, tags and search help find or coordinate work but never grant access to linked records. A role title or Admin overview does not automatically allow task reassignment or self-approval; capabilities and alternate approvers come from authorized settings.

Each department has its own settings, procedures, supported registers, task templates and access scope. Shared teams can participate in a project where the configured responsibility matrix says they apply. No department inherits access simply because another department was merged into the same workspace.

### Department ownership and multiple lead roles

Department identity, workspace grouping, lead assignment and stage RACI are separate settings linked by stable IDs. A department owns its registers and work. One active person may lead multiple departments, such as Service and IT or Operations and HR; assign that person to each required department in Settings. Do not duplicate people or merge records. CFT/PM belongs to Operations; the confirmed enclosure/3D manufacturing stages belong to MMF.

The role editor must support selecting people without keyboard modifiers, because one person may hold multiple roles. It must surface active roles with a missing L1–L4 level or no assigned member as setup gaps. Never infer a lead from an account's display name, department, or local demo roster.

Explicit department assignments grant only configured capabilities and record scope. RACI selects Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed departments; it does not grant access. Resolve task owners through those departments' lead/member assignments. Grouping workspaces grants no permissions. Independent review and alternate approvers remain required where applicable, including personal requests when one person leads both departments.

Authorized leads edit their permitted stages and default tasks; Admin manages shared department/lead mappings and cross-department configuration. Keep ten fixed company phases TG-0–TG-9. Stages, dependencies, default tasks, deliverable mappings and RACI are versioned settings beneath them. New work uses the accepted configuration; existing projects keep their pinned version until a reviewed update. A lead change requires explicit audited handling of pending tasks/reviews and preserves historical signatures. Saved settings must feed all authorized portal views and the Android contract through one shared source, with refreshed views after saving.

Acceptance: assign one lead to two departments and reload; verify both permitted workspaces, related record creation, generated task ownership, draft/upload options and scoped review actions; deny an unrelated lead. Verify tasks generate once from eligible stages/defaults and RACI. Verify pending work and attribution after changing the lead. Verify local, shared TEST and Android separately. The [multi-track roadmap](Electronics%20Multi-Track%20Lifecycle%20and%20APQP%20Roadmap.md) owns lifecycle details and handoffs.

## 3. Project lifecycle and work automation

Use **Project → Phase → Stage → Default Task**. The company has exactly ten fixed phases, TG-0 through TG-9, with the names in the roadmap's [canonical phase table](Electronics%20Multi-Track%20Lifecycle%20and%20APQP%20Roadmap.md#canonical-tg-0-to-tg-9-phase-names), reconciled from the New Hexa source. Phase IDs and names are fixed; a tollgate is the recorded decision at a phase boundary, not another work level. Department stages and their default task templates are configurable beneath those phases; no legacy stage names or internal track numbering create extra company phases. In Settings, selecting a phase filters the visible stages to that phase. Admin controls shared department mappings, lifecycle versions and default stage durations. Authorized leads manage their scoped stage/default-task definitions and RACI, then configure project-specific task timelines during feasibility. Preserve stable IDs and version history.

The current shared TEST checkpoint records 110 stages and 478 default tasks: all 82 workbook source stages plus 28 supporting stages. These figures describe the TEST configuration, not a production guarantee. The lifecycle roadmap owns detailed phase names and crosswalks.

Sales flow: capture an **Enquiry**; dispatching it creates/updates the **Opportunity** and starts configured multi-department **Feasibility** work. Departments provide scoped inputs and evidence. Only after the authorized feasibility decision is the engineering track selected and the **Project** created. Do not ask users to choose a track at enquiry intake or create a project before feasibility approval.

Supported engineering scopes are Hardware only, Firmware only, Software only, Hardware + Firmware, Firmware + Software on existing hardware, Hardware + Firmware + Software, and Ready-made deployment. Optional PoC/pilot intent is separate from the technical scope. Applicable QA, procurement, manufacturing, Finance, Service/IT and HR/OPS work can run in parallel when configured dependencies allow.

When a stage becomes eligible, create only its configured default tasks for applicable departments, exactly once, from the project's pinned lifecycle/RACI/settings version. Do not silently add a second hard-coded task catalogue. Show why each default task applies. Task completion may prepare a stage for review; it never approves a stage or passes a tollgate. Only an authorized, attributable and audited decision advances gated work. Retries are idempotent; blocked or missing evidence stays visible.

### Feasibility budget and task timeline

Admin configures the default target and allowed duration range for each stage beneath fixed TG-0–TG-9 phases; phase targets are rollups. Admin may configure task-duration/effort defaults as suggestions. During feasibility, each participating department lead sets the project-specific planned start, finish, duration and effort for applicable tasks, then saves that schedule with the feasibility baseline. Suggest task windows from configured task defaults, dependencies and the parent stage's planned window. The stage window is a hard boundary: every task and its dependency-aware schedule must fit within the stage and enclosing phase; overlap is allowed only when dependencies permit. Never extend a stage automatically to fit tasks. If timing defaults are absent, ask the lead for estimates rather than inventing an equal split. If a schedule exceeds its stage window, show the conflict for lead review and authorized stage-duration change; do not silently alter either duration. A stage-duration change proposes a recalculated task schedule, while the accepted feasibility plan remains pinned until an authorized update is saved. Later settings or organization-chart changes must not silently rewrite accepted schedules, assignments or history. Task timers record actual elapsed effort separately from estimates and never complete tasks or pass a tollgate. Finance/Admin must configure approved rates before effort can be shown as currency. The [lifecycle roadmap's scheduling section](Electronics%20Multi-Track%20Lifecycle%20and%20APQP%20Roadmap.md#stage-durations-feasibility-estimates-and-task-assignment) owns detailed defaults, feasibility editing, rescheduling, timing and cost-estimate rules.

Default task assignees use configurable organization-chart role profiles: L1 lead, L2 senior/specialist, L3 associate/technician, and L4 operator/trainee. Each task template names the intended profile level; Settings maps that profile to an active person within the owning department. Do not silently substitute a different level. If the intended level is unmapped or has multiple eligible assignees, leave assignment pending for the department lead to resolve. The profile/level is the stable default, while an authorized person can change an individual assignment. Existing tasks and signed history do not silently change after settings edits.

These four level meanings are the canonical organization-chart vocabulary owned here; Admin configures department roles and maps active members to levels in Settings. Departments may omit levels they do not need, and one person may hold multiple department roles. The lifecycle and sample-data plans consume this model without redefining it.

The fictional employee roster, stable fixture keys, sample contact/profile fields and org-chart test coverage are owned by the [Sample Data and Test Plan](Feature%20Roadmap/Sample%20Data%20and%20Test%20Plan.md#canonical-employee-and-organization-chart-sample-roster). Runtime team, demo profile and directory views must derive from that single roster; it never replaces live organization Settings or contains real employee data.

The [Electronics Multi-Track Lifecycle and APQP Roadmap](Electronics%20Multi-Track%20Lifecycle%20and%20APQP%20Roadmap.md) owns detailed phase names, track applicability, stage/task crosswalks, scheduling, timers and handoffs. This master owns shared company rules for organization, department ownership, lead roles, access, RACI and cross-portal behavior. Both plans require versioned settings and pinned project baselines; changes must not silently rewrite accepted schedules, assignments or approvals.

## 4. Operational systems and procedures

- **Project and department work:** one project identity across overview, timeline, tasks, requirements, records, documents, decisions and reports. Department views show only authorized relevant work; project-wide views obey the same access checks.
- **Procurement and inventory:** keep purchase requests, suppliers, quotations, purchase orders, receiving, BOM and inventory as distinct workflows. Goods/services requests go to Procurement first. Stock changes only through authorized receipt/issue/transfer/adjustment transactions. A digital file or reusable design never changes stock quantity.
- **Finance and expenses:** direct request choices are Travel, Reimbursement and Purchase. Travel and reimbursement route to Finance; goods/services requests route to Procurement before any Finance review. Keep one Finance category field; do not duplicate it with a business-purpose field. Project/stage context may suggest a value, but the employee chooses or edits it. Do not invent company limits, tax rules or approval caps.
- **Budgets and settlements:** Finance configures periods, categories and controls. Department allocations and transfers are versioned, reasoned and approved by a separate authorized person. Requests, reservations, invoices, payments and reimbursements must not double-count spend. Employees attach actual receipts/evidence; Finance reviews and records settlement/payment references. No employee or lead approves their own personal request; route to an authorized alternate.

### Project economics, spend and growth reporting

Show project economics from linked, traceable inputs: feasibility estimates and tentative BOM costs as forecasts; purchase commitments and receipts as committed cost; settled expenses/invoices as actuals; and customer-approved quotation, order or revenue records as commercial value. Keep projected and realized values separate, identify currency and reporting period, and calculate margin only when its required revenue and cost inputs are present. Never turn a draft estimate, unapproved quote, task timer or demo fixture into recognized revenue, actual spend or profit. If inputs are missing, label the metric incomplete instead of inventing a value. Growth views may summarize enquiry-to-opportunity-to-project conversion, booked workload, revenue and margin trend only from their canonical records and clearly named statuses. Auto-generated business cases, quotations, budget summaries and review documents must retain source links, assumptions and Draft state until the responsible Finance/business approver records a decision.
- **Services:** a shared, settings-managed company/service catalogue covers approved e-waste, cloud/IT, engineering software, lab/testing and other company services. Authorized department leads maintain their own services; Finance/Admin govern shared expense categories.
- **Quality:** QA owns applicable criteria, reviews, nonconformance/CAPA disposition and release authority. Certification applicability and evidence are project-specific; no simulated certificate or result is accepted as proof.
- **Service and IT:** distinct ticket types may appear in one configured workspace. Tickets can request scoped help from another department without exposing protected client/project/asset details or changing ticket status. Repeated matching tickets may be highlighted as candidates for human technical review; frequency alone does not diagnose a cause or create an approved product change.
- **People and Operations:** use authorized people/team records for capacity, competency, training, onboarding and relevant safety/compliance readiness. Keep confidential HR data restricted. CFT/PM work belongs to Operations.

## 5. Records, documents and reusable assets

The document catalogue is the single source for the 38 document families and their mappings. Generate editable drafts from verified project, stage, people and settings data; show provenance and leave unknown/conflicting values for authorized review. An auto-draft is not an approved document.

The [consolidated generation and role Docs Vault plan](SSM%20One%20Document%20Catalogue%20-%20Auto-Drafting%20Audit%20and%20Native%20Upload%20Rules.md#consolidated-generation-and-role-docs-vault-plan) owns role-wise profiles, TG-0–TG-9 deliverables, customer intake bindings, output formats and audit coverage. Each role's Docs Vault and the central index show the same canonical documents with scoped access. Company, role, policy, template and source-record changes refresh live indexes and flag affected drafts for reviewed regeneration; issued documents keep their original revisions until superseded. Lead/member assignments, RACI, document signers and workspace grouping remain separate configurable relationships. The supplied drafting specification is reconciled to the configured departments and stage IDs; its 25-role count, sample limits and illustrative data do not override Settings. Completion requires profile-by-role/stage coverage and saved-state verification, not a document count.

Minimize effort with one contextual record/document dialog: infer project, current phase and eligible stage; record type determines its fixed stage rule. Allow draft entry, original-file upload or authorized reuse in the same flow. Common documents can be created without a project; project deliverables require one. Keep originals immutable and drafts/revisions distinct. Upload, reuse, task completion or a generated template never implies approval, release, certification or customer acceptance.

PCB, harness, enclosure/3D, firmware and documents may be reused by linking an exact compatible source revision. Derivatives preserve source, project, stage, revision and consumer lineage; changing the source does not silently change existing projects. Physical inventory is tracked separately from digital design/document files.

### Multi-role smart upload and intelligent document ingestion

The [Multi-Role Smart Upload and Intelligent Document Ingestion Specification](References/SCMS%20Portal%20%E2%80%94%20Multi-Role%20Smart%20Upload%20%26%20Intelligent%20Document%20Ingestion%20Specification.docx) is the detailed supporting specification for portal-wide Smart Upload and contextual role upload workbenches. It is linked to this master and the document catalogue, not an additional competing plan. This is planned capability; inclusion here does not establish implementation or live verification.

Provide a global Smart Upload entry and contextual role actions using one ingestion service. Follow five steps: select the authorized use-case profile; parse supported files/OCR/tables; match extracted entities to current shared masters; let the user inspect source and editable values with validation/interlocks; then commit reviewed records and generate permitted downstream drafts. Show unsupported formats, extraction failures, missing values and ambiguous matches explicitly. Engineering binaries/packages retain original file identity and hashes; accepting a file does not imply its technical contents have been validated.

The source specification owns the detailed 25-profile use-case matrix covering SOR/RFQ and customer orders, BOMs, component datasheets/catalogues, vendor quotations, invoices, receiving, dispatch, engineering/test outputs, manufacturing/EOL, service/RMA, HR, legal, document control, administration, client and R&D inputs. Map these use-case profiles to configured departments, roles and permissions: they are not 25 new departments. Retain this master's L1–L4 hierarchy; source L0/L0–L3 labels require explicit mapping, and TST maps to EMF's EOL scope. Do not create or grant roles from an uploaded document.

Match customers, suppliers, projects, parts, approved-source records, purchase orders and GRNs through stable canonical IDs in the shared authorized directories. Provide searchable selectors and deliberate resolution for missing/ambiguous entities; never match solely by similar names or silently overwrite master values. Bind imported requirements, PMSS fields, BOM rows, inventory items and generated drafts to the selected project/stage/record and exact source revision. Preserve current inventory categories, tabs, quantities and assignment controls while adding ingestion entry points.

Support single/batch uploads and a source-versus-fields review view with page/clause/table-row provenance, editable extracted values, units/currency, matching status and interlock explanations. Retain the original file, digest, extraction version, reviewer, timestamps and approved field changes. Retries must not duplicate records, masters, line items or generated drafts. Imported text is data and cannot instruct the system to change permissions, approvals or business rules.

Priority connected flows are SOR to reviewed intake/requirements; BOM/datasheet to reviewed parts and sourced BOM/shortfall drafts; and quotation to comparison, budget review and a PO draft. Receiving and invoice ingestion link the same PO, supplier and line-item IDs for GRN and three-way matching. Extend the same pipeline to every authorized role use case in the reference. Draft generation never issues a PO, approves a quotation, certifies a result, changes stock or clears a lifecycle gate automatically. Each such transition retains its independent authorized business transaction and approval. Thresholds, statutory rules, role mappings, templates and source-specific field counts must be reconciled with accepted lifecycle/catalogue/settings versions before activation; do not turn illustrative source values into universal company policy.

Acceptance requires source-to-field traceability, searchable shared master connectivity across departments, ambiguous/absent master handling, scoped multi-role access, original/hash retention, batch retry idempotency, human review, draft-versus-issued boundaries and authorized downstream handoffs. Verify unsupported/corrupt files and conflicting revisions fail visibly without partial commits. Test the complete SOR, BOM/datasheet, quotation-to-PO and invoice/GRN flows in Shared TEST before claiming completion; platform subplans must reference this shared contract.

## 6. Shared interface and settings

Keep the existing portal theme, responsive behavior and working controls. Prefer compact headings, one clear destination per workflow, searchable project/department selectors and on-demand descriptions. Overview is the entry point; Tasks, Projects, Team, Registers and Documents have clear destinations. Checklist, Kanban and Table are views of the same authorized tasks, not separate task stores. Completed tasks start collapsed; review/approval guards remain in force. Training lifecycle simulations belong under Help/Training, separate from operational status.

Settings are the source for department registry, phase/stage templates, default tasks, RACI, expense categories, services and applicable department procedures. An authorized settings change is versioned, permission-checked, impact-previewed and audited. Each client must read the same saved configuration; no duplicate hard-coded catalogs that drift from Settings.

### Feature visibility and change-safety rule

Do not hide, replace or downgrade an established feature through a role-, environment- or route-specific fallback without first stating the reason, affected roles and conditions, expected duration, user impact and restoration path. Keep the full authorized feature available; apply read-only restrictions only when the actual permission policy requires them, and explain which actions are unavailable and why. Preserve existing data and behavior by default. For temporary changes, define an expiry or completion condition and rollback before applying them; verify the normal route and restricted route separately. A restricted view must not silently stand in for the full feature. This rule applies across web and Android, including inventory, lifecycle, documents and approvals.

Android implements the same authorized feature behavior using mobile-appropriate layouts; visual structure need not match web. Keep mobile screens focused and avoid duplicating web configuration editors. Read the same saved settings, role profiles, stable IDs and project-pinned revisions through the shared backend contract. The Android plan owns only its mobile interaction and verification details and links back to the shared decisions here and in the lifecycle roadmap.
